Module 1 • Lesson 3✂️ Select & Mask Mastery: The Hard Cutout

The Module 1 deliverable, and the true test of everything so far. Flowing, curly hair is the hardest common cutout there is. 💇 The Goal: Every Strand Survives

Curly hair against one background, then lifted cleanly onto another, individual flyaway curls intact. On the right, the original studio shot; on the left, the same subject cut out and placed on a new backdrop. Drag the handle and inspect the edge:

🧠 Mental Model: A Pipeline, Not a Tool

Beginners look for the one button that cuts out hair. There isn't one. A professional hard cutout is a short pipeline, where each step cleans up what the previous one could not:

  1. Rough in the whole subject with Select Subject or Quick Selection — get the solid mass right, ignore the wisps.
  2. Refine the hairline with the Refine Edge Brush, which rebuilds fine strands.
  3. Rescue anything still missing with a channel mask (Lesson 1.2) painted into the layer mask.
  4. Decontaminate the edge so the old background's color lets go.

No single step is hard. The skill is knowing the order and not expecting step one to do step three's job.

🛠️ Guided Build: The Hard Cutout

Open a portrait with loose or curly hair against a reasonably plain background. Save as cutout-working.psd, 16-bit. Duplicate the layer so the original stays intact beneath.

Step 1: Rough in the mass · 6 min

  1. Select ▸ Subject. It nails the body and solid hair, and gives up on the wisps. That is expected.
  2. Tidy obvious misses with Quick Selection (paint to add, Alt/⌥ to subtract). Do not chase strands yet.

Step 2: Refine the hairline · 10 min

  1. Click Select and Mask. Set View to On Layers or the red Overlay.
  2. Take the Refine Edge Brush and paint along the wispy hairline (not the solid interior). Photoshop rebuilds the fine strands and their partial transparency.
  3. Keep Smooth and Feather low; nudge Shift Edge slightly negative.

⚠️ Refine the edge, not the middle

Dragging the Refine Edge Brush across solid hair turns it translucent and mushy. Stay on the boundary between hair and background; that is the only place it belongs.

Step 3: Rescue gaps with a channel mask · 8 min

  1. Output the current result to a New Layer with Layer Mask. Inspect: some flyaways may still be missing.
  2. From Lesson 1.2, build a quick channel mask of the hair (pick the channel with the most hair-vs-background contrast, push with Levels), then paint that detail into your layer mask with a low-opacity brush to bring lost strands back.

Step 4: Decontaminate & pressure-test · 8 min

  1. If you did not already, re-enter Select & Mask and tick Decontaminate Colors, raising Amount until the old background's color leaves the edge.
  2. The test: drop a temporary saturated color layer behind the cutout. Any surviving fringe screams against it. Fix, then place your real new background. Module 1 deliverable: done. 🏆

✅ The standard

A believable hair cutout keeps the fine strands, shows no colored halo against a garish test background, and has a soft (not razor) edge. Hit those three and your composites will hold up anywhere.

✅ Project Completion Checklist

  • ☐ Solid mass selected, then the hairline refined with the Refine Edge Brush
  • ☐ Missing strands rescued via a channel mask painted into the layer mask
  • ☐ Edge decontaminated; no fringe against a saturated test background
  • ☐ Output as a layer + mask; original kept as a hidden backup
  • ☐ Subject placed convincingly on a new background

🧗 Now You: Solo Variation

🌟 Your challenge

  1. Cut out a subject shot against a busy background (not a plain studio wall). This is where the channel-mask rescue step earns its place.
  2. Cut out something non-human and tricky: a glass, a potted fern, a pet. Each stresses a different part of the pipeline.
  3. Place one cutout on a dark background and one on a light background. Decontamination that looks fine on light often reveals a halo on dark.

Going further: keep the whole thing non-destructive by outputting to a Smart Object, so you can re-open Select & Mask and adjust the refinement later without starting over.

🍳 Recipe Card: Hard Cutout

Extract flowing hair

  1. Select ▸ Subject for the solid mass
  2. Select & Mask → Refine Edge Brush along the hairline; low Smooth/Feather, Shift Edge slightly negative
  3. Rescue missed strands with a channel mask painted into the layer mask
  4. Tick Decontaminate Colors; output to New Layer with Layer Mask
  5. Test against a saturated background; fix any fringe

Mantra: rough in, refine, rescue, decontaminate — in that order.

📝 Module 1 Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • A hard cutout is a pipeline: rough in, refine, rescue, decontaminate.
  • The Refine Edge Brush rebuilds wisps; a channel mask rescues what it misses.
  • Decontaminate Colors strips the old fringe so the edge adopts any scene.
  • Always pressure-test the edge on a saturated background.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Select Subject already did the hair. Do I still need all this?

Its automatic hair is good and getting better, and for easy cases it is enough. This pipeline is for the hard cases it fumbles — dark hair on a dark background, fine flyaways, busy backgrounds — and for when you need to refine the result by hand rather than accept the automatic guess.

My cutout looks fine on white but has a halo on dark backgrounds.

Classic under-decontamination. Raise the Decontaminate Colors amount, and consider a tiny inward Shift Edge. Always test on the darkest background the cutout will actually sit on.
