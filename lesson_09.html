Module 3 • Lesson 3✨ Eyes, Teeth & Hair — Natural Finish

The Module 3 deliverable. The last five percent of a retouch — brighter eyes, cleaner teeth, tamed stray hairs — is what people actually notice. Done with restraint it lifts a portrait; overdone it screams "edited." This lesson is about knowing exactly where to stop.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Brighten and sharpen eyes with a masked local edit
  • Whiten teeth by removing yellow first, then brightening — not the reverse
  • Tame flyaway hair and fill gaps without a helmet-hair look
  • Apply every finish through a mask so nothing bleeds where it shouldn't
  • Judge the "natural line" — the point where more becomes worse

⏱️ Estimated Time: 55 minutes

🎯 Project (Module 3 deliverable): A finished portrait taken through the full pipeline — even skin, sculpted light, and natural eye/teeth/hair polish — that a stranger would call "a great photo," not "a great retouch."

👤 The Goal: The Last Five Percent

The skin is untouched here — this is only the local polish, and it is still enough to make the face read as more awake and alive. Drag the handle:

🧠 Mental Model: Local, Masked, Restrained

Every finishing move shares one shape: make an adjustment, then hide it behind a black mask and paint it back only where it belongs. The eyes get brighter only in the eyes; the teeth lose yellow only on the teeth. Nothing global, everything local.

The second rule is restraint. Eyes and teeth are the classic tells of an amateur edit because they are so tempting to push. The trick for teeth is counter-intuitive: remove the yellow first (desaturate) and only then brighten. Brightening a yellow tooth gives you a bright yellow tooth; pulling the yellow out and lifting gently gives you a clean, believable one.

🔧 The Three Finishes

Each finish is a masked adjustment layer or a dedicated retouch layer. Here is the recipe for all three:

⚠️ The eyes are not toothpaste-white

Real eye whites are faintly grey or pink and real teeth are faintly warm. If you push either to pure white, the brain instantly flags it as fake. Aim for "clean," never "glowing." Back off until you doubt you did anything, then stop there.

🛠️ Guided Build: Finish the Portrait

Open your sculpted portrait from Lesson 3.2. This is the final stage of the Module 3 pipeline.

Step 1: Eyes · 12 min

  1. Add a Curves layer, lift the midtones a little. Invert the mask to black (Ctrl/⌘+I), then paint white with a small soft brush over the iris and the whites.
  2. Optionally add a merged, High-Pass-sharpened layer masked to just the iris for crispness. Keep the catchlight sacred — never paint it out.

Step 2: Teeth · 10 min

  1. Add a Hue/Saturation layer. Choose Yellows, drop saturation; then on Master, raise lightness slightly. Yellow out first, brightness second.
  2. Invert the mask to black and paint the effect onto the teeth only. Feather the gum line so there is no hard edge.

Step 3: Hair · 10 min

  1. Add an empty layer, Clone/Heal set to Sample: All Layers. Remove distracting flyaways against the background with short strokes that follow the hair direction.
  2. Fill a thin gap by cloning nearby strands along their natural flow. Leave a few stray hairs — perfectly smooth hair looks like a wig.

Step 4: Final review · 8 min

  1. Zoom out to fit and flip the canvas horizontally (Image ▸ Image Rotation ▸ Flip Horizontal) — a mirrored view exposes anything overdone. Flip back.
  2. Toggle all your Module 3 work off and on. If the "before" looks untouched and the "after" just looks like a good day, you have nailed the natural line. Deliver. 🏆

✅ Project Completion Checklist (Module 3 deliverable)

  • ☐ Even skin from frequency separation (3.1)
  • ☐ Form restored with dodge & burn (3.2)
  • ☐ Eyes brightened and sharpened through a mask
  • ☐ Teeth de-yellowed then gently lifted, masked
  • ☐ Flyaways tamed, a few strays left, on their own layer
  • ☐ Passes the flip-and-mirror test — reads natural, not retouched

🧗 Now You: Solo Variation

🌟 Your challenge

  1. Finish three portraits back to back and time yourself. A professional headshot finish should take minutes, not hours — speed comes from a fixed routine.
  2. Make a deliberately over-finished version (glowing eyes, blue-white teeth, plastic hair) next to your restrained one. Keep both as a reminder of the natural line.
  3. Finish a portrait meant for a magazine (push a little) and the same one for a LinkedIn headshot (push almost nothing). Context sets the amount.

Going further: save your three finishing layers as a template group you can drag into any portrait, then just repaint the masks. Your future self will thank you in Module 6.

🍳 Recipe Card: Natural Finish

Eyes, teeth, hair — masked and gentle

  1. Eyes: Curves lift + optional High-Pass sharpen, black mask, paint iris & whites
  2. Teeth: Hue/Sat — drop Yellows, raise lightness slightly, black mask, paint teeth
  3. Hair: empty layer, Clone/Heal (All Layers), remove flyaways, fill gaps
  4. Keep whites off-white and teeth faintly warm
  5. Flip horizontal to audit, toggle before/after, deliver

Mantra: the best finish is the one no one can point to.

📝 Module 3 Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Frequency separation splits tone from texture so you fix each without harming the other.
  • Dodge & burn on a 50% grey Soft Light layer rebuilds three-dimensional form, built low and slow.
  • Finishing is local, masked, and restrained: brighten eyes, de-yellow then lift teeth, tame hair.
  • The mark of a pro retouch is that you cannot see the retouch — the natural line governs everything.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

What order do these three lessons go in on a real job?

Always: global corrections → frequency separation (clean) → dodge & burn (sculpt) → eyes/teeth/hair (finish). Cleaning after sculpting undoes your light; finishing before cleaning wastes effort. The pipeline order is the workflow.

How much retouching is "too much" ethically?

Technique is neutral; context sets the line. Evening skin and brightening eyes for a headshot is normal; reshaping a body for an ad aimed at teenagers is a different conversation. Keep the person recognizably themselves, and be honest with clients about what was changed.
