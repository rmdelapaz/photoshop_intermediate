Module 3 • Lesson 2🔦 Dodge & Burn Sculpting

Even, clean skin can still look flat. Dodge (lighten) and burn (darken) let you paint with light itself — deepening shadows and lifting highlights to sculpt the face back into three dimensions, the way a painter or a studio light would.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain why dodge & burn adds form that smoothing can't
  • Build a non-destructive 50% gray Soft Light dodge & burn layer
  • Work at low flow to build light gradually instead of streaking it on
  • Sculpt the planes of a face: cheekbones, jaw, nose bridge, brow
  • Check your work in black & white to judge form without color

⏱️ Estimated Time: 50 minutes

🎯 Project: Take the retouched portrait from Lesson 3.1 and sculpt its light so the face gains depth without any obvious brush marks.

👤 The Goal: Bring Back Form

The same rounded form, twice. On the right it is lit flatly — you can barely tell it is a sphere. On the left, a little burn in the core shadow and a dab of dodge on the highlight give it obvious volume. Dodge & burn does exactly this to a face. Drag the handle:

🧠 Mental Model: Painting With Light

A camera flattens a face; our brains read depth from where light falls and where it doesn't. Dodging lightens — it pulls a plane toward the viewer. Burning darkens — it pushes a plane back. By exaggerating the light that the lens already captured, you rebuild the volume the flattening took away.

The professional habit is low and slow. You do not lighten a cheekbone in one stroke; you brush over it many times at very low flow, each pass adding a whisper of light, until the transition is so smooth there is no edge to see. Streaky dodge & burn is just impatient dodge & burn.

🧠 The spiral from the intro course

The intro course (Module 3.3) introduced dodge & burn as sculpting light. Here you take it to production depth: a planned light map that follows the bone structure, black-and-white checks, and coarse and fine layers you can revise for a client.

🩶 The 50% Gray Layer

The Dodge and Burn tools edit pixels directly and are hard to undo. Pros instead paint on a separate layer filled with 50% gray set to Soft Light. On Soft Light, mid-gray is invisible; paint slightly lighter than gray and it dodges, slightly darker and it burns. Every stroke is on its own layer — endlessly adjustable, fully reversible, and maskable.

✅ Check form in black & white

Color hides uneven sculpting. Drop a temporary Black & White adjustment layer on top while you dodge & burn — with color removed, lumps and streaks in the light become obvious, and you fix them before anyone sees them in color.

🛠️ Guided Build: Sculpt the Face

Open skin-retouched.psd from Lesson 3.1 and click the collapsed retouch group itself (not a layer inside it) so the new layer lands above the retouch, outside the group. A layer created inside the group would inherit the group's reduced opacity. Clean skin first, sculpt light second, always in that order.

Step 1: Add the gray layer · 3 min

  1. Layer ▸ New ▸ Layer, Mode Soft Light, tick Fill with 50% gray.
  2. Grab a soft round brush, Hardness 0, Flow 5–10%, Opacity 100%. Foreground white for dodge, black for burn (tap X to swap).

Step 2: Burn the recesses · 12 min

  1. With black, build up shadow gently in the eye sockets, along the sides of the nose, under the cheekbones, and under the jaw.
  2. Many light passes, not one heavy one. Let the shadow deepen slowly until the plane reads as receding.

Step 3: Dodge the highlights · 12 min

  1. Swap to white. Lift the tops of the cheekbones, the bridge and tip of the nose, the center of the forehead, and the brow bone.
  2. Follow the light that is already there — you are amplifying it, not inventing a new light source.

Step 4: Judge and temper · 6 min

  1. Toggle a temporary Black & White layer on top to check the form is smooth, then turn it off.
  2. Lower the gray layer's opacity until the sculpting is felt but not seen. Subtlety is the mark of good D&B.
  3. Delete the temporary Black & White layer and save as portrait-sculpted.psd. Lesson 3.3 finishes this file. 🏆

✅ Project Completion Checklist

  • ☐ A 50% gray Soft Light layer (non-destructive)
  • ☐ Recesses burned, highlights dodged, following the real light
  • ☐ Built at low flow — no visible streaks or edges
  • ☐ Checked in black & white and corrected
  • ☐ Layer opacity tuned so the effect is subtle
  • ☐ Saved as portrait-sculpted.psd for Lesson 3.3

🧗 Now You: Solo Variation

🌟 Your challenge

  1. Take one flatly lit portrait and dodge & burn it into three different lighting moods: soft beauty, dramatic side light, and gentle top light. Same face, sculpted light only.
  2. Sculpt something that is not a face — a leather bag, an apple, a car panel. Product retouchers live on dodge & burn; the principle is identical.
  3. Deliberately over-dodge until it looks chalky, then rescue it with opacity and masking. Learn where "too much" begins.

Going further: keep two separate gray layers — one for big global light shaping, one for tiny detail (individual lashes, a catch-light). Pros almost always split D&B into coarse and fine.

🍳 Recipe Card: Dodge & Burn

Sculpt with a gray layer

  1. New layer → Soft Light, fill 50% gray
  2. Soft brush, Flow 5–10%, white = dodge, black = burn
  3. Burn the recesses (sockets, sides of nose, under jaw)
  4. Dodge the highlights (cheekbones, nose bridge, brow)
  5. Check form with a temporary Black & White layer
  6. Lower layer opacity until it's felt, not seen

Mantra: build light in whispers, and the sculpting stays invisible.

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Dodging lifts a plane toward the viewer; burning pushes it back. Together they rebuild form.
  • Work on a 50% gray Soft Light layer so every stroke stays editable and maskable.
  • Paint at Flow 5–10% and build light in many passes, following the light already in the photo.
  • Check form under a temporary Black & White layer, then lower opacity until it is felt, not seen.

🎉 What You've Accomplished

Clean skin (3.1) plus sculpted form (3.2) gives you a portrait that already looks professional. You can now shape light on a face, or on any product, without leaving a brush mark, and revise it whenever a client asks.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Should I use the Dodge/Burn tools instead of the gray layer?

For quick, throwaway edits the tools are fine. For any work you might revise (i.e., all client work) the gray layer wins because it is non-destructive, maskable, and lets you dial the whole effect up or down after the fact.

How do I keep dodge & burn from looking overdone?

Two rules: paint at very low flow so nothing lands in one stroke, and finish by lowering the layer's opacity. If a client can point to your sculpting, it is too strong.
