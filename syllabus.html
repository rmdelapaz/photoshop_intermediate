Photoshop Intermediate: From Practitioner to Pro

The hands-on sequel to an introductory Photoshop course. Over eight sessions you mask by tone and channel, manage color for screen and print, retouch and composite to a commercial standard, build design systems, automate, and merge multi-shot images, finishing one professional deliverable every session, then plan, build, and present a client-style capstone.

How the Course Runs

One module, one 3-hour session, one finished deliverable. The course is built as eight modules, and every module already ends in a finished professional piece: a hard cutout, a graded image delivered for screen and print, a retouched portrait, a composite key visual, a multi-size campaign, a data-driven batch, a set of multi-shot merges, a presented capstone. So the schedule follows the modules instead of cutting across them. Each session runs every lesson's framing, live demo and guided build, with a quick self-check; each lesson's solo variation starts in class and finishes at home, so nobody goes home with half a deliverable.

The last two sessions share the capstone. Module 7 has two lessons, so Session 7 ends with a capstone kickoff: you write your brief in class and leave with a moodboard and plan started, then build most of the piece as homework. Session 8 is for plan review, a final studio block, the case study, presentations, and peer critique.

Weekly: one session a week for eight weeks. The week between sessions is for the solo variations on your own images and your learning journal.
Twice a week: four weeks. Keep at least two days between Sessions 7 and 8 for the capstone build.
4-day intensive: two sessions a day (morning and afternoon). Run Session 7 on the morning of Day 4 only if learners can build overnight; otherwise schedule Session 8 a few days later.

Core Craft Sessions 1–3 (Modules 1–3) Commercial Work Sessions 4–6 (Modules 4–6) Going Further Sessions 7–8 (Modules 7–8)

Course Details

Requirements

  • Working Photoshop skills: layers, masks, selections, Camera Raw, Curves, Smart Objects, and basic retouching and compositing (the introductory course or equivalent)
  • A Windows or macOS computer that runs Adobe Photoshop 2025 or newer (the current release is recommended); the desktop app, not Photoshop on the web or iPad
  • An Adobe account with a plan that includes Photoshop, and access to Adobe Fonts
  • Internet access and generative credits for Session 6 (generative tools run in the cloud)
  • A mouse or pen tablet; 16 GB of RAM strongly recommended for layered 16-bit files

What's Provided

  • A setup guide to complete before Session 1
  • The full 23-lesson course workbook (yours to keep)
  • Practice files for every lab, so you are never stuck without a suitable image
  • The online Recipes II library: 28 quick, standalone how-to cards
  • Guided builds in every session, a capstone studio, and peer critique
  • A finished deliverable from every session, plus a presented capstone with a case study

Bring Your Own Images

  • S1: a high-contrast landscape; a bare tree or power lines against a bright sky; a portrait with loose or curly hair on a fairly plain background
  • S2: a favorite photo to grade (a portrait is ideal), a second shot from the same set in different light, and one deliberately vivid image
  • S3: a portrait of someone who has agreed to be retouched and shown in class (a self-portrait works), large original, teeth and loose hair visible
  • S4: a background with clear perspective and visible shadows, and a clean cutout from your Session 1 work
  • S5: your Session 4 key visual as key art; optional: your own logo as a transparent PNG
  • S6: your layered Session 4 composite (or a cramped photo), a folder of 8–12 mixed landscape and portrait copies, and a short list with photos you may use
  • S7: a simple layered design to animate (your Session 5 master works); one or two capstone ideas with reference images
  • S8: your capstone master, brief, moodboard and pass screenshots, built as homework from images you own or have the right to use

Assessment

  • Session deliverables: each module's finished piece, checked against its lesson's Project Completion Checklist
  • Learning journal: a short entry after every lesson, revisited in the case study
  • Capstone: briefed, planned, built, presented, and scored on a published 12-row rubric drawn from the lessons' own checklists: brief and plan; meets the brief, composition, focal point, tonal structure; built in passes and file hygiene; the case study; and the presentation
  • Completion: every session deliverable submitted, plus a capstone with no rubric row below 2 of 3, the deliverables exported at spec, and a layered master
  • Below the bar? Targeted feedback and a resubmission
