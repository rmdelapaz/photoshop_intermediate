L13 · Type Systems, Styles & Grids

Q1
With a 16 px base and a 1.25 ratio, which set of sizes does the lesson's modular scale give you?
C) 13 / 16 / 20 / 25 / 31 / 39
Why: multiply 16 by 1.25 repeatedly (20, 25, 31.25, 39.06) and divide once (12.8), rounding to whole pixels. The lesson assigns Caption 13, Body 16, Subhead 20, H2 25, H1 31, Display 39. The other options are even steps (A, B) or doubling (D), not a single ratio.
Q2
The client wants all body text one step larger across a styled layout. What makes this a single edit?
A) Editing the Body Paragraph Style once, so every instance updates
Why: a style bottles the full spec under a name; edit the style and every instance re-flows. That is "the difference between a document and a system." Retyping sizes (B) is the eyeball-each-label habit the lesson replaces; Image Size (C) scales everything, not just body.
Q3
How do you build a baseline grid in Photoshop?
D) Preferences ▸ Guides, Grid & Slices: Gridline Every = your body leading, Subdivisions 1
Why: Photoshop has no dedicated baseline-grid feature. The document grid set to the body leading (for example 24 px for 16 px body) is the workaround, shown with View ▸ Show ▸ Grid (Ctrl/⌘+'). New Guide Layout (A) makes columns and rows; there is no "Snap To Baseline" (B), and styles don't draw grids (C).
Q4
Snap To ▸ Grid is on, but your text block's baselines still don't sit on the gridlines. Why, and what do you do?
B) Snapping works on layer edges, not baselines; zoom in and align the first baseline by eye with the arrow keys
Why: the lesson is explicit that View ▸ Snap To ▸ Grid snaps a layer's edges, not its baselines. With leading equal to the grid increment (or a multiple), once the first baseline sits on a gridline every following line lands on the grid too. Auto leading (D) is usually 120% of the size and won't match the grid.
Q5
Your chosen typeface has no Bold weight, and your H1 style needs one. What does the lesson say to do?
C) Choose a different face that ships a real Bold
Why: "If a typeface ships a real Bold or Italic, use that; if it doesn't, choose a different face." Faux Bold (A) algorithmically smears the regular weight and looks cheap up close; a Stroke style (B) is the same trick by another name.
Q6
What does View ▸ Guides ▸ New Guide Layout create?
A) Columns and rows, with gutters and margins, but not a baseline grid
Why: New Guide Layout (older versions: View ▸ New Guide Layout) gives the horizontal division of the page. The baselines come from the document grid in Preferences. Neither creates styles (C) or artboards (D).
Lab reference: A Type System

A correct result: a PSD whose Paragraph Styles panel lists one named style per role (Display, H1, H2, Subhead, Body, Caption, or similar) plus at least one Character Style (Emphasis or Link). Sizes match a written scale (13 / 16 / 20 / 25 / 31 / 39, or the copy deck's ×3 version: 39 / 48 / 60 / 75 / 93 / 117). Column guides from New Guide Layout are visible, and the document grid (Ctrl/⌘+') is set to the body leading (24 px, or 72 px at ×3) with Subdivisions 1. The first baseline of each text block sits on a gridline. Two families only; real OpenType on (ligatures visible in "fi"/"fl"); Faux Bold and Faux Italic off. Editing the Body style's size re-flows every body block. Compare with labs/module-5/instructor/l13-system-layout-INSTRUCTOR.png.

How to check fast: click a body text layer and look at the Paragraph Styles panel. A style name with a "+" means a local override; no name highlighted means the text isn't styled at all. Toggle the grid and zoom to 200% on one block.

Common wrong results and why:

  • One block doesn't update when the style changes: it was sized by hand after the style was applied (an override). Clear the override.
  • Baselines drift off the grid on line two: leading isn't the grid increment or a multiple of it (often left on Auto).
  • Sizes like 18 / 22 / 28 / 36: freehanded, not from the scale. Ask for the written base and ratio.
  • Type sizes look wildly wrong in the Character panel: Units & Rulers ▸ Type still in points while the plan is in pixels (or vice versa).
  • A "bold" heading that looks smeared: Faux Bold on a regular weight.
  • Four families: the ransom note again. Back to one display, one text.

L14 · Smart-Object Templates & Linked Assets

Q1
You warped a Smart-Object placeholder and added clipped shadow layers. What happens to that treatment when you use Replace Contents?
B) It stays on the container and applies to the new art
Why: the container holds the treatment; the contents are the variable. Transforms, warps, filters and styles on the Smart Object carry straight over to the new art, which is what makes templates possible.
Q2
What is the key difference between an embedded and a linked Smart Object?
D) A linked one points to one file on disk that many documents can share; an embedded one is stored inside the PSD
Why: embedded is portable and self-contained, but each copy is independent (one-off protection). Linked (File ▸ Place Linked) is the backbone of a multi-file system: edit the one file and every document using it updates. Both scale without loss and both take Smart Filters (A, B); it is linked, not embedded, that follows the source (C).
Q3
After Replace Contents, the new design lands too big and misaligned. What's the most likely cause?
A) The new art has different pixel dimensions, and the container's transform is applied to it
Why: the container keeps its transform and applies it to whatever you swap in. Export replacement art at the placeholder's exact pixel size, or double-click the placeholder and paste the new design into its existing canvas.
Q4
Where should a mockup's shadows and highlights go?
C) On Multiply and Screen layers above the placeholder, clipped to it
Why: "Effects on the container, not the art." Painting shadows into the art (A) or inside the Smart Object (B) means every new design needs them again, or they get swapped out with the contents.
Q5
You're sending a project with linked assets to a colleague. What should you do first?
B) File ▸ Package, which collects the linked files with the document
Why: Package gathers all linked files so nothing breaks on another machine. Renaming the files (C) is exactly what breaks links; rasterizing or flattening (A, D) destroys the template.
Q6
You tidied your desktop and moved logo.png into a new folder. What happens in the documents that link it?
D) Photoshop loses the link and the layer shows a warning until you relink it
Why: move or rename a linked file and Photoshop loses it. Right-click the layer ▸ Relink to File to reconnect. The lesson's discipline: keep linked assets in a project folder you don't reshuffle.
Lab reference: A Reusable Template

A correct result (poster wall): above l14-mockup-poster-wall.jpg, a Smart Object (badge on the thumbnail) whose contents are 1200 × 1800 px, warped so its corners meet the blank poster's corners (about (930, 250), (1530, 290), (1522, 1172) and (940, 1216) in the 2400 × 1600 scene). Above it, a Multiply layer carrying the fold creases and the diagonal window-bar shadow, and a Screen layer for the lit fold ridges, both clipped (indented with a down-arrow). Replace Contents with any …-1200x1800.png design lands exactly on the poster with the same folds and shadow; the 1800 × 1200 landscape design lands distorted or misaligned, which is the intended lesson. Tote bag: same structure, with Warp bending the art into the fabric folds and a Multiply layer carrying the diagonal crease. Link test: a document with logo.png placed linked (link icon on the layer) that refreshes after the source is changed or replaced with l14-logo-v2.png (same 1600 × 600 px, now teal and gold with a coffee-bean mark).

How to check fast: ask the learner to run Replace Contents with a different design while you watch. If the treatment holds, the template works.

Common wrong results and why:

  • Shadows darken the wall around the poster: the Multiply layer isn't clipped to the placeholder.
  • New art comes in at half size: the placeholder was drawn at about 600 × 900 (the poster's on-screen size), not the art's 1200 × 1800.
  • No Smart Object badge: the rectangle was never converted, or was rasterized. Rebuild the placeholder.
  • Warp and shadows gone after a swap: they were applied inside the Smart Object (on the art), not on the container.
  • Linked logo doesn't update: it was placed embedded, or placed from a different copy (Downloads) than the one that was edited.
  • "Can't save as PNG with layers": the learner added a layer to logo.png. Edit its single layer directly, or swap the file in the file manager.

L15 · Campaign from One Master

Q1
Your campaign has a print poster, a square, a story and a banner. Which do you design first, and why?
A) The print poster, the largest, so smaller sizes scale down from it
Why: "Design at the biggest size, export down." Scaling a small master up for print softens everything. Biggest first, always.
Q2
How should the logo get onto all four artboards so one edit updates every one?
C) File ▸ Place Linked into each artboard (so every copy points to the same file)
Why: linked Smart Objects mean the shared art exists once and appears everywhere. Embedded copies (B) and pasted pixels (A) are independent; editing the source changes nothing. Export As (D) only renders what is already on each artboard.
Q3
What renders every artboard to its own file in one pass?
B) File ▸ Export ▸ Export As with all artboards (or File ▸ Export ▸ Artboards to Files)
Why: Export As renders each artboard to its own file, with format and scale (1×, 2×) set in one dialog. Artboards to Files is the batch alternative. Package (D) gathers linked files for handoff; it doesn't export images.
Q4
How do you adapt the poster design to the 3:1 banner?
D) Anchor the focal point first, then spread the elements horizontally around it
Why: recompose, don't squash. Keep the focal point and shared elements, and let the layout breathe differently: stack for the story, center for the square, spread for the banner. Scaling, stretching or cropping the poster (A–C) gives a banner that isn't designed for its shape.
Q5
The printer needs the poster in CMYK, but the social sizes must stay RGB. What's the right approach?
A) Keep the master RGB and make the CMYK conversion a separate, final step on an exported copy
Why: every artboard in a document shares one color mode and one resolution, so (B) is impossible, and converting the whole master (C) degrades the screen sizes. Export As does no CMYK conversion (D).
Q6
You edited and saved the linked logo file, but one artboard still shows the old version. What do you do?
C) Layer ▸ Smart Objects ▸ Update All Modified Content
Why: that is the lesson's fix when an artboard doesn't refresh on its own. Placing the logo again (A) creates a second copy and defeats the system.
Lab reference: The Campaign (Module 5 deliverable)

A correct result: campaign-master.psd (RGB) in the project folder with four artboards: Poster 3300 × 5100, IG square 1080 × 1080, Story 1080 × 1920, Banner 1500 × 500. On each, the logo and key art are linked Smart Objects (link icons) pointing at files in the same folder; headline and subhead use the L13 Paragraph Styles (the style name shows in the panel). Each artboard is recomposed: poster stacked (key art large, headline, subhead, CTA, logo), square centered, story stacked with all text clear of the top ~250 px and bottom ~340 px bands, banner spread horizontally with key art on one side and headline plus CTA on the other. Export As has produced four files in one pass. The learner can change the linked logo (or key art), save, and show all four artboards updating, then re-export. Compare with labs/module-5/instructor/l15-campaign-reference-INSTRUCTOR.png.

How to check fast: open the Layers panel and expand two artboards: the same linked filename should appear in both. Then watch the one-edit proof.

Common wrong results and why:

  • Soft poster: the poster artboard was built small and scaled up, or the key art was enlarged past 100% (the practice PNG is 2400 px wide).
  • Only some artboards update: the logo was embedded (or pasted) on those; or the update needs Update All Modified Content.
  • Banner is a shrunken poster: squashed, not recomposed.
  • Story text under the UI: safe zone ignored. Drop l15-safe-zones-story.png on top to check, then delete it before export.
  • Type sizes differ in spirit across artboards: styles weren't brought in; text was set by hand. Copy a styled layer in from the L13 file.
  • Missing links on reopen: files moved or were saved outside the project folder. Relink, or Package next time.
  • Master converted to CMYK: screen exports look dull. Undo; CMYK is for an exported copy only.
