Advanced Selection & Masking

In the intro you painted masks by hand and let Select Subject find edges. Now the image does more of the work: luminosity masks built from its own brightness, channel masks built from one color's brightness, Blend-If that hides by tone, and a four-step pipeline for the hardest common cutout. Deliverable: a flawless cutout of flowing hair on a new background.

Before You Start: Your Professional Baseline

Not a lesson, nothing to deliver: the setup habits the whole course assumes. Tick them off in the Session 1 setup check.

  • ☐ Know your working space. Edit ▸ Color Settings: my RGB working space says ____________________. (Don't change it yet: Module 2 goes deep on this.)
  • ☐ Work in 16-bit when you can. Image ▸ Mode ▸ 16 Bits/Channel before building masks. It can't add detail an 8-bit file never had, but it stops new banding as you edit.
  • ☐ Diagnose before you edit. Window ▸ Histogram: look where the tones sit before you touch a slider.
  • ☐ Calibrate your monitor if you do color-critical work, and set up shortcuts for the tools you reach for most.
  • ☐ I can open the Channels panel (Window ▸ Channels). It's today's main stage.
When "it's not doing anything," run the four-point check

1. Which panel am I in (Layers or Channels)? 2. Which channel or layer is highlighted? 3. Which thumbnail has the border (image or mask)? 4. Is a selection active (marching ants, or the "not visible" warning)? Nearly every stuck moment in this module is one of these four.

L01

Luminosity Masks: Select by Tone (1.1)

55 min
By the end you can
  • Explain a luminosity mask: a selection built from the image's own luminance
  • Load a Lights or Darks selection from the channels
  • Adjust highlights and shadows independently, edges self-feathered and invisible
  • Narrow a mask to a tonal band (just the brightest highlights) by intersecting
Key ideas
  • The image paints the mask. Load a Lights mask and the bright pixels become white (selected, editable) while the dark pixels become black (protected). The mask is a grayscale copy of the photo's luminance.
  • The edges are perfect. The mask follows the actual tones, so its transitions are exactly as soft as the light in the scene: no halos, no hand-feathering.
  • It's self-targeting. The sky is the bright part, so a Lights mask already contains it. No painting around the horizon.
  • Invert Lights and you have Darks. Intersect Lights with itself and the selection slides into the brightest tones only.
  • Why two curves beat one: a single S-curve brightens the sky while it lifts the land. Two masked curves move highlights and shadows in opposite directions at once.
In the Channels panel, on the RGB thumbnailDoes
Ctrl/⌘-clickLoad Lights (a new selection)
Ctrl/⌘ + Shift-clickAdd to the current selection
Ctrl/⌘ + Alt/⌥-clickSubtract from the current selection
Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + Alt/⌥-clickIntersect: keep only what both share
Worked example: rescue the landscape
  1. Open a high-contrast photo (or l01-high-contrast-landscape.jpg). Image ▸ Mode ▸ 16 Bits/Channel, then Save As luminosity-working.psd.
  2. Diagnose, then load Lights. Read the histogram: highlights piling near the right, shadows near the left. In Channels, Ctrl/⌘-click the RGB thumbnail.
  3. Tame the highlights. With the selection active, add a Curves adjustment layer (the selection becomes its mask) and pull the curve down to bring the sky back. Spilling onto the land? Undo the layer, reload Lights, intersect once or twice, then add Curves again.
  4. Lift the shadows. Reload the RGB luminosity selection, invert it (Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + I), add another Curves layer, and lift the shadows.
  5. Check and finish. Zoom to 100% along the horizon: no seam, no glow. A glow means the masks overlap: reduce one layer's opacity or re-intersect. Optional gentle global Curves on top. Save the layered master.
Recipe card: Luminosity Mask
  1. Channels panel: Ctrl/⌘-click RGB = Lights selection
  2. Add a Curves layer → the selection becomes its mask
  3. Too broad? Intersect (Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + Alt/⌥-click RGB) to reach extreme tones
  4. Shadows: reload, invert (Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + I), add another Curves
  5. Check the horizon at 100% for seams; work in 16-bit

Mantra: let the image paint the mask.

Your turn 2: Glow, midtones, and a head-to-head

On a portrait or still life (or l01-still-life-window-light.jpg): (a) load Lights and add a subtle glow by lifting only the brightest highlights. Do the shadows change? (b) Build a Midtones mask: save Lights and Darks as channels (Select ▸ Save Selection), then Select ▸ All and Ctrl/⌘ + Alt/⌥-click each saved channel to subtract it; add contrast to only the mids. (c) On your landscape, compare a luminosity-masked sky darken with a hand-painted one.

Think: why is Lights minus Darks not a midtones mask? What does it select instead?

Watch out

Ctrl/⌘-click the RGB channel in Channels, not the layer thumbnail in Layers (that loads the whole image). Add Curves while the selection is active, or the mask comes out white. "No pixels are more than 50% selected" just means the ants can't show; the selection is still there.

L02

Channel Masking & Blend-If (1.2)

60 min
By the end you can
  • Read the Red, Green & Blue channels as ready-made grayscale masks
  • Turn the highest-contrast channel into a selection (duplicate, push with Levels)
  • Use Blend-If to drop out a layer's lights or darks instantly
  • Pick the right technique for the subject: luminosity, channel, Blend-If, or a blend of all three
Key ideas
  • Three masks you already have. Every RGB image is three grayscale images stacked: Red, Green, Blue. Each is a potential mask. You shop for the one with the most subject/background separation.
  • Skies are blue, so the Blue channel keeps a sky bright while dark branches stay dark. (For skin, Blue is where blemishes stand out most: same habit, different job.)
  • Forge, don't paint: duplicate the winning channel, then push the copy toward black and white with Image ▸ Adjustments ▸ Levels. Adjustment layers can't target a channel.
  • White loads as the selection. Against a bright sky, the sky loads first, so invert to get the subject.
  • Blend-If (Layer Style ▸ Blending Options) hides a layer's pixels by tone in real time. This Layer white slider inward drops the layer's lights; black slider inward drops its darks. Alt/⌥-drag to split a slider for a soft blend.
  • When to use which: Blend-If is instant and non-destructive but tone-global. A channel mask is editable and spatial: you can paint on it afterward.
Worked example: extract a tree
  1. Open a bare tree, power lines, or other intricate subject against plain bright sky (or l02-bare-tree-sky.jpg). 16-bit, Save As channel-working.psd.
  2. Channel-shop. In Channels, click Red, then Green, then Blue. Pick the one where the subject is darkest and the background brightest.
  3. Forge the mask. Drag the winner onto the New Channel icon. With only the copy selected, Image ▸ Adjustments ▸ Levels (Ctrl/⌘ + L): pull the black and white points inward until the sky is white and the tree black, fine twigs still intact. Paint stray gaps with a hard black or white brush on the channel.
  4. Load it and cut. Ctrl/⌘-click the copy, invert (Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + I), click RGB, go to Layers with the image layer active, and add a layer mask. Drop a new sky behind it (l02-new-sky.jpg) to check the edges.
  5. A Blend-If finish. Place a texture (l02-grunge-texture.jpg) as a new layer on top. Blending Options: pull the This Layer white slider left until only the darker parts remain; Alt/⌥-split for a soft blend. No mask at all.
Recipe card: Channel Mask
  1. Channels panel: click R / G / B, pick the highest-contrast one
  2. Duplicate it → Image ▸ Adjustments ▸ Levels to force near black & white
  3. Clean gaps with a hard brush on the channel
  4. Ctrl/⌘-click to load → invert (the white background loads first) → click RGB → add layer mask
  5. Fast alternative: Blend-If to drop lights or darks by tone (Alt/⌥-split to feather)

Mantra: shop the channels before you paint.

L03

Select & Mask Mastery: The Hard Cutout (1.3)

60 min
By the end you can
  • Run the full Select & Mask workflow, tuned for hair
  • Rebuild wispy strands with the Refine Edge Brush
  • Rescue what refinement misses with a channel mask (Lesson 1.2)
  • Kill the fringe with Decontaminate Colors and pressure-test the edge
Key ideas
  • A pipeline, not a tool. There is no one button for hair. Each step cleans up what the previous one couldn't.
  • Rough in the solid mass (Select Subject, Quick Selection) → refine the hairline (Refine Edge Brush) → rescue missing strands (channel mask painted into the layer mask) → decontaminate the edge (the old background's color lets go).
  • Refine the edge, not the middle. The Refine Edge Brush across solid hair turns it translucent and mushy.
  • Decontaminate last. It rewrites edge pixels, so it can only output to a new layer or document.
  • The standard: fine strands kept, no colored halo against a garish test background, a soft (not razor) edge.
Worked example: the hard cutout
  1. Open a portrait with loose or curly hair against a reasonably plain background (or l03-curly-hair-portrait.jpg). 16-bit, Save As cutout-working.psd. Duplicate the layer so the original stays intact beneath, and hide the original.
  2. Rough in the mass. Select ▸ Subject. Tidy obvious misses with Quick Selection (paint to add, Alt/⌥ to subtract). Don't chase strands yet.
  3. Refine the hairline. Select ▸ Select and Mask (Ctrl/⌘ + Alt/⌥ + R). View: On Layers or Overlay. Edge Detection: tick Smart Radius, raise Radius a few pixels; try Refine Hair. Refine Edge Brush along the hairline. Smooth and Feather low; Shift Edge slightly negative.
  4. Rescue gaps. Output To: New Layer with Layer Mask (Decontaminate off). Build a channel mask of the hair (best channel → duplicate → Levels), Ctrl/⌘-click it, invert if needed. Click the layer mask thumbnail; paint white with a soft, low-opacity brush along the hairline.
  5. Decontaminate & pressure-test. With the rescued mask selected, reopen Select and Mask, tick Decontaminate Colors, raise Amount, output to New Layer with Layer Mask. Put a saturated color layer behind the cutout; fix any fringe; place your real background (l03-new-background.jpg). Save.
Recipe card: Hard Cutout
  1. Select ▸ Subject for the solid mass
  2. Select ▸ Select and Mask → Smart Radius on, Refine Edge Brush along the hairline; low Smooth/Feather, Shift Edge slightly negative; output New Layer with Layer Mask
  3. Rescue missed strands: load a channel mask as a selection, paint white into the layer mask
  4. Final pass: reopen Select and Mask on the rescued mask, tick Decontaminate Colors, output to New Layer with Layer Mask
  5. Test against a saturated background; fix any fringe

Mantra: rough in, refine, rescue, decontaminate, in that order.

Watch out

Hide the original layer before judging the edge: if it's visible underneath, every cutout looks perfect. Refine Edge Brush on the boundary only. Leave Decontaminate off until the final pass. The test color layer goes below the cutout. A layer "vanished"? Decontaminate hid it; check its eyeball.
