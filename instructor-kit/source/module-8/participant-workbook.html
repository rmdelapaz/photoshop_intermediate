L21

The Brief & the Plan (8.1)

45 min · Session 7 kickoff + home
By the end you can
  • Write a tight creative brief: objective, audience, deliverables, constraints, success criteria
  • Translate a vague idea into specific, checkable requirements
  • Build a moodboard that fixes the visual direction before you start
  • Draft a staged plan that maps each phase to the skills you have learned
  • Scope realistically so the project is ambitious but finishable
Key ideas
  • The brief is the contract between the vision and the work, with a client or with your future self at 2 a.m. wondering if you are done. Its job is to make success checkable.
  • Specificity is the whole game. Pin every vague adjective to a reference image, a measurable spec, or a named constraint.
  • Idea → brief → plan. Each step removes ambiguity, so by the time you open Photoshop there are no unanswered questions to stall you.
  • A moodboard of 6–12 annotated references fixes the visual target while it is still cheap to change, and becomes the yardstick you grade drafts against.
  • Scope for finishing, not for showing off. A tightly executed simple idea beats an ambitious half-finished one. Stretch goals must be cuttable.

L22

Studio Session: Build It (8.2)

self-paced · home + Session 8 studio
By the end you can
  • Execute a project in passes: block-in, refine, polish
  • Keep ruthless layer hygiene: groups, names, color labels
  • Stay non-destructive with Smart Objects and adjustment layers throughout
  • Version and save so you can always step back safely
  • Use fresh-eyes tricks to judge your own work honestly
Key ideas
  • Work in passes. Raise the whole image a level at a time. Each pass is done across the frame before you start the next, never one perfect corner at a time. Zoom out between passes and judge the frame, not the pixel.
  • Layer hygiene. Group by role, top-to-bottom in render order, name everything, color-label groups. Every adjustment a live layer, every scaled element a Smart Object, every filter a Smart Filter.
  • Save versions, and save often: capstone_v01, v02 at each pass, plus a flattened "reference" JPG of milestones.
  • Done is when a pass changes almost nothing and every brief success criterion is met, not when you run out of things you could do.

Watch out
  • One perfect corner. Hours at 300% on a detail you may later cut. Finish the pass across the frame first.
  • File slow and huge? Save a full layered version first. Then: convert heavy element groups to Smart Objects (or linked ones), rasterize truly final elements you're sure of, Edit ▸ Purge ▸ Histories, and keep working resolution sensible. Good hygiene from Pass 1 is the real fix.
  • Check layer left on. Toggle the Black & White layer off before you export.
  • Moving things a pixel back and forth? You're past done: save it and present it.
Push it further (optional)
  • An alternate grade or a day/night variant from the same master (Modules 4.3, 5.2)
  • Spin the hero into a sized campaign set (Module 5.3) or a short animated version (Module 7.2)
  • Record the repetitive export as an Action or data set (Module 6)
Recipe card: Studio Session
  1. Pass 1 block-in (place, scale, group + name early)
  2. Pass 2 refine (color, retouch, light/shadow, masks, type)
  3. Pass 3 polish (atmosphere, unifying grade, details)
  4. Stay non-destructive; save versions each pass
  5. Fresh-eyes check, then grade against the brief

Mantra: raise the whole image a level at a time, and keep every decision reversible.

L23

Present & Case Study (8.3)

50 min · Session 8 + home
By the end you can
  • Structure a case study: challenge → approach → process → result → reflection
  • Present the final piece in context with mockups, not floating in space
  • Use before/after and progress shots to show your thinking
  • Write about your work clearly, for clients and employers
  • Package and export a portfolio-ready presentation
Key ideas
  • Pretty pictures say "I can operate Photoshop." Case studies say "I can solve problems." Show the thinking, not just the pixels.
  • Show the decisions, not every step. A highlight reel, not a tutorial: the three or four moments that show judgment.
  • Context tells the reviewer what the thing is for and makes your work look shipped rather than sketched. Use the Smart-Object mockups from Module 5.2.
  • Keep before/after honest. A fabricated "before" is obvious to anyone who does the work. Disclose any generative assistance.
Worked example: the case-study page, top to bottom
  1. Hero shot: the finished piece, big, first. Earn the scroll.
  2. The brief in a line: the challenge and who it was for, from Lesson 8.1.
  3. Approach: your strategy in a sentence or two: the key idea that solved it.
  4. Process: 3–5 progress shots or a before/after that reveal how it was built.
  5. Details: a few close crops of craft you're proud of (a clean cutout, the grade, the type).
  6. Result: deliverables in context/mockups, plus any outcome you can claim.
  7. Reflection: one honest paragraph on what you learned.
Step 1: Assemble the assets (in class)

Export a clean hero, 3–5 progress shots, a couple of detail crops, and a before/after. Place the hero and deliverables into mockups for context. No mockup of your own? Use l23-mockup-wall.jpg (framed print), l23-mockup-phone.jpg (social feed), or l23-mockup-sleeve.jpg (album sleeve).

  1. Draw a rectangle over the gray area where the art goes and Convert to Smart Object. Rotate or Warp it to match the surface if needed.
  2. Double-click its thumbnail, place your hero in the tab that opens, save, and close.
  3. Add the surface's shadows (Multiply) and highlights (Screen) above it, clipped. Swap art later with Layer ▸ Smart Objects ▸ Replace Contents.
  4. Export firstname-context.jpg and firstname-before-after.jpg to the shared folder.
Step 2: Write the story (in class)

Keep each beat tight, a few sentences. Lead with the outcome or the idea, not the tools: "A warm, editorial key art that reads on mobile" beats "I used Curves and Generative Fill."

Steps 3–4: Lay it out, export & publish (at home, within a week)
  • Build the page top to bottom: hero → brief → approach → process → details → result → reflection, in your Module 5 type system.
  • Export web-sized images via File ▸ Export ▸ Export As, and a PDF via File ▸ Save a Copy ▸ Photoshop PDF (or File ▸ Export ▸ Artboards to PDF if you laid it out on several artboards). Automate the export if you built an Action (Module 6).
