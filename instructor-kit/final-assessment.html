Capstone: Brief, Build & Case Study

The single end-of-course assessment (Module 8). Each learner writes a one-page brief for a self-directed professional project, builds it in passes in a clean, non-destructive file, and presents it as a case study: the work plus its story. Score it with the rubric below; completion requires the deliverables exported at spec and a saved layered master.

Covers Modules 1–8 Brief written in Session 7 · presented in Session 8 Case study published within 1 week Pass: 2+ on every row

The Brief

Choose one project from the list below (the same four options as Lesson 8.1: The Brief & the Plan) and take it from a one-page brief to a published case study. A strong capstone is a single, real-feeling deliverable. Scope for finishing, not for showing off: a tightly executed simple idea beats an ambitious half-finished one every time, and stretch goals must be cuttable without breaking the deliverable.

Images, consent, and disclosure

Learners use their own photos, the kit's practice files, or stock whose license and credit terms they have checked. The course website's example images are licensed to the author and must not be used. Portraits need the subject's consent to be retouched and shown. Any Generative Fill or Expand is disclosed in the case study's process section; Content Credentials may be attached on export as well.

Required deliverables
  • The brief and plan (Lesson 8.1): a one-page brief with all six parts (objective, audience, deliverables + specs, constraints, success criteria, references/moodboard), an annotated moodboard of 6–12 references, and a staged plan mapping skills, assets, and milestones.
  • The layered master (Lesson 8.2): a grouped, named, color-labeled, non-destructive file, plus incremental versions (capstone_v01.psd, v02…) saved along the way.
  • The deliverables (Lesson 8.2): every output named in the brief, exported at spec.
  • The case study (Lesson 8.3): hero first, the five beats (challenge, approach, process, result, reflection), an honest before/after and 3–5 progress shots, deliverables in context via mockups; laid out with the learner's type system, exported (web images and a PDF), and published. Presented in Session 8; the finished layout is published within one week.

Grading Rubric

Every row quotes the lessons word for word. Row 1 is the Lesson 8.1 checklist. Rows 2–5 are Judging Your Own Work from Lesson 8.2: the four fresh-eyes checks and "grade against the brief," the same rows learners self-score in the participant workbook. Rows 6–7 are the Lesson 8.2 checklist (process and file hygiene). Rows 8–11 are the Lesson 8.3 case-study checklist. Row 12 scores the Session 8 presentation. Score each row 1–3; the maximum is 36. A workbook "strong" corresponds to 2 or 3, "not yet" to 1.

Pass = no row below 2, plus the deliverables exported at spec and a saved layered master.

Flipped, the piece tips to one side or a leftover element grabs attention. Balanced in both orientations; nothing stray. Every element earns its place; the layout reads with intent either way round.
4 · Focal point & read /3"Zoom to fit and squint — if the focal point and read survive at thumbnail size and blurred, the structure is sound." At thumbnail size or squinted, nothing wins, or the room names the wrong focal point. The focal point and the read survive at thumbnail size and blurred. Instant at any size: strong, simple shapes, and type (if any) that still reads as a thumbnail.
5 · Tonal structure /3"Desaturate to check — a temporary Black & White adjustment layer at the very top of the stack… exposes tonal problems that color hides." In black and white the subject merges with the background, or pasted-in elements have the wrong values. Clear tonal separation in black and white; element values agree with the scene. Values deliberately lead the eye, with depth from light to dark.

Completion policy

A learner completes the course when every session deliverable has been submitted and the capstone meets the pass rule: no row below 2, plus the deliverables exported at spec and a saved layered master. A high total with one row at 1 does not pass. Score rows 1–7 and 12 in Session 8 (from the brief, the file, and the presentation) and rows 8–11 when the published case study arrives, within one week. Below the bar, give targeted feedback tied to the rows above and allow one resubmission within two weeks.

Presentations & Peer Critique

The Session 8 protocol from the Module 8 facilitator guide: about 4 minutes per learner, 50 minutes for a class of about 12. Hand out printed rubric cards (rows 2–5 and 8–10) to reviewers, and ask each presenter to put their one-line brief on screen.

Each presenter
  1. Present (90 s): the hero in context (a mockup), the brief in a line, the before/after, and the 30-second spoken story: challenge, approach, result.
  2. Squint test (15 s): the whole room squints; two or three people call out what they see first. Does it match the brief's focal point?
  3. Presenter notes (15 s): write the grow in the workbook's critique box and say thank you.
Two reviewers (90 s), assigned in advance
  1. Read the presenter's brief first: "Which success criterion does it meet most clearly?"
  2. Pick one rubric row: one glow (what works, specifically, pointing at the pixels).
  3. One grow, phrased "I wonder if…" or "What if you tried…"
Keep critique kind and specific

Critique the work, not the person; point at the pixels; judge against the presenter's brief, not your taste; the presenter listens and says thank you, no defending; kind is not the same as vague. For 13 or more learners, run the same protocol in pods of 4 to 5 for 35 minutes, then a 15-minute whole-room showcase of one piece per pod. For groups under 7, give each presenter 6 to 7 minutes and let everyone review.
