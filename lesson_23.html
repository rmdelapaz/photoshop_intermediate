Module 8 • Lesson 3🏆 Present & Case Study

The course finale. A finished image in a folder is not yet a portfolio piece — a case study is. Telling the story of the challenge, your approach, the process, and the result is what turns good work into work that gets you hired, and it is the last skill this course gives you.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Structure a case study: challenge → approach → process → result → reflection
  • Present the final piece in context with mockups, not floating in space
  • Use before/after and progress shots to show your thinking
  • Write about your work clearly, for clients and employers
  • Package and export a portfolio-ready presentation

⏱️ Estimated Time: 50 minutes

🎯 Project (course capstone): A complete case study of your capstone — the artwork, its story, and its results — ready for a portfolio or a client.

👤 The Goal: The Work Plus Its Story

🧠 Mental Model: Tell the Story

A portfolio full of pretty pictures says "I can operate Photoshop." A portfolio of case studies says "I can solve problems." Reviewers and clients hire the second one, because every real job is a problem, not a picture. The case study proves you understood a goal, chose an approach, executed it, and can judge the result.

The arc is simple and always the same: Challenge (what needed solving), Approach (your strategy and why), Process (how you built it, with progress shots), Result (the finished work in context), and Reflection (what worked, what you would change). Show the thinking, not just the pixels.

🧱 Anatomy of a Case Study

Lay it out top to bottom like a story. A workable structure:

  • Hero shot — the finished piece, big, first. Earn the scroll.
  • The brief in a line — the challenge and who it was for, from Lesson 8.1.
  • Approach — your strategy in a sentence or two: the key idea that solved it.
  • Process — 3–5 progress shots or a before/after that reveal how it was built.
  • Details — a few close crops of craft you're proud of (a clean cutout, the grade, the type).
  • Result — deliverables in context/mockups, plus any outcome you can claim.
  • Reflection — one honest paragraph on what you learned.

⚠️ Show the decisions, not every step

A case study is a highlight reel, not a tutorial. Pick the three or four moments that show judgment — the tricky mask, the grade choice, the composition call — and skip the routine. Reviewers spend seconds; make each one earn its place.

🖼️ Presenting in Context

An image floating on white reads as an exercise; the same image in context reads as real work. Use the Smart-Object mockups from Module 5.2 to place your poster on a wall, your campaign in a phone feed, your album on a record sleeve. Context tells the reviewer what the thing is for, and it makes your work look shipped rather than sketched.

✅ Keep before/after honest

Before/after and progress shots are persuasive precisely because they're true. Show the real starting point and the real steps; a fabricated "before" is obvious to anyone who does the work and costs you all your credibility. Your honest process is impressive enough.

🛠️ Guided Build: The Case Study

Gather your capstone artwork, the brief and moodboard from 8.1, and the progress screenshots you saved during 8.2.

Step 1: Assemble the assets · 12 min

  1. Export a clean hero, 3–5 progress shots, a couple of detail crops, and a before/after.
  2. Place the hero and deliverables into mockups for context.

Step 2: Write the story · 15 min

  1. Write the five beats: challenge, approach, process, result, reflection. Keep each tight — a few sentences.
  2. Lead with the outcome or the idea, not the tools. "A warm, editorial key art that reads on mobile" beats "I used Curves and Generative Fill."

Step 3: Lay it out · 12 min

  1. Build the case-study page top to bottom: hero → brief → approach → process → details → result → reflection. Use your Module 5 type system for a clean, consistent read.

Step 4: Export & publish · 8 min

  1. Export the case study (PDF and web-sized images, or a page). Automate the export if you built an Action (Module 6).
  2. Put it where people will see it — portfolio site, PDF, a post. The capstone, and the course, are complete. 🏆

✅ Project Completion Checklist (course capstone)

  • ☐ A big, clean hero shot leading the piece
  • ☐ The five story beats written tight and outcome-first
  • ☐ Honest before/after and 3–5 progress shots
  • ☐ Deliverables shown in context via mockups
  • ☐ Laid out with your type system, exported, and published

🧗 Now You: Ship It

🌟 Finish the job

  1. Publish the case study somewhere public and share it. Shipping is a skill; practice it here.
  2. Write a 30-second spoken version of the story — the answer to "tell me about this piece" in an interview.
  3. Turn your best two or three earlier lessons' projects into mini case studies so this capstone isn't alone in your portfolio.

Going further: ask someone in the field for honest feedback on the case study, then revise. The willingness to seek and act on critique is the growth habit that outlasts any single technique.

🍳 Recipe Card: Case Study

Work plus story

  1. Lead with a big hero; earn the scroll
  2. Five beats: challenge · approach · process · result · reflection
  3. Show honest before/after + 3–5 progress shots
  4. Present deliverables in context (mockups)
  5. Lay out with your type system; export & publish

Mantra: show that you think like a professional, not just that you can push pixels.

🎓 Course Summary & Farewell

🏁 What You Have Learned — The Whole Course

You arrived able to operate Photoshop. You leave able to work like a professional. Here is the ground you covered:

  • Module 1 — Advanced Selection & Masking: luminosity and channel masks, Blend-If, and flawless hard cutouts.
  • Module 2 — Color & Tone, Deep: color management, Curves and gradient-map grading, LUTs, and print-safe soft proofing.
  • Module 3 — High-End Retouching: frequency separation, dodge & burn sculpting, and a natural finish that never looks retouched.
  • Module 4 — Commercial Compositing: matched perspective, light, shadow, atmosphere, and one unifying grade.
  • Module 5 — Design Systems & Type: type systems, Smart-Object templates, and a full campaign from one master.
  • Module 6 — Generative + Automation: generative assist, conditional Actions, and data-driven graphics with a taste of scripting.
  • Module 7 — Multi-Shot Techniques: panoramas, HDR, focus stacking, and Timeline animation.
  • Module 8 — The Capstone: brief, plan, disciplined execution, and a portfolio-ready case study.

🎉 You Can Now…

Cut anything out cleanly, grade for any screen or press, retouch a portrait invisibly, build a believable composite from nothing, run a consistent design system, automate volume work, combine many frames into one, and present it all like a pro. That is a genuinely professional toolkit — and, just as importantly, you built the judgment to know which tool a problem needs and when to stop.

🌱 Keep Growing

  • Build your portfolio — turn each capstone-quality project into a case study.
  • Practice deliberately — pick one weak spot and drill it until it's a strength.
  • Study work you admire — reverse-engineer the grade, the light, the type. Naming why it works is how taste grows.
  • Seek critique — the growth mindset that carried you through this course is what carries a career.
  • Specialize or broaden — go deep on retouching, compositing, or design, or widen into motion and 3D. You have the base for any of them.

❓ A Last Question

What should my very next project be?

Something slightly beyond comfortable, briefed the way you learned in 8.1, finished and presented the way you learned in 8.3. Growth lives just past the edge of what you're sure you can do — and now you have the process to get there safely.
